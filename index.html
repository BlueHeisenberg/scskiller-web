Why it stutters

The frame waits for a compile.

When a game meets a new effect, your GPU driver has to compile its shader right then, and the frame waits for it. That's the hitch.

SCSKiller compiles them before you play.

How it works

Pick your games. Press Compile.

Three steps, with the game closed.

01

Scan

Finds your games and the shaders they ship with.

  • Games grouped by store
  • Shader and pipeline counts, cache size, compile time
  • A status such as Ready to compile or Warmed
  • A Play button that starts the game through its store
02

Plan

Works out which pipelines each game will build.

  • Frame times of your last session, with the shader stutters told apart from other hitches (recorder on)
  • Warmed for the current driver
  • Pipeline and shader counts, cache size, compile time
  • The optional recorder switch

Gaps get filled by an optional recording while you play. Anti-cheat games are never launched, injected into, or given the recorder.

03

Compile

Builds them into your driver's cache, where the game finds them.

  • Progress and pipelines per second
  • Games wait their turn in the queue
  • Finished games stay listed with what failed or was skipped

Library, game details and compile queue. Sample data.

Limits

What it fixes, and what it can't.

Fixes

Shader compilation stutter.

Can't fix

Traversal and streaming stutter, or hitches with other causes.

~Now and then A shader it couldn't find or record can still compile in game.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Anti-cheat games?

SCSKiller never launches them, injects into them, or gives them the recorder. It only reads their files.

Does it run while I play?

It doesn't need to. The game reads the driver's cache on its own.

Will Windows warn me?

Probably, the first time. The first release isn't code-signed yet; signing is in progress. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Does it touch game files or drivers?

It doesn't patch either. Only the optional recorder touches a game folder: it installs a small d3d12.dll and its settings file, and writes its recording, a log and the frame times next to the game. Turning it off removes what it installed; uninstalling SCSKiller removes the rest.

What about driver updates?

An update clears the shader cache. SCSKiller notices and compiles again, on its own if you let it.

What about Intel GPUs?

Not supported yet: I don't have one to test on. If you'd like to sponsor an Intel Arc GPU, get in touch at contact@scskiller.com.

Do I need an account?

No. Signing in with Patreon only adds the supporter features.
